# The opportunity

## Lowering the distance between making a game and finding an audience

A browser can make a game immediately accessible. Distribution still involves more than putting a build online: a creator needs a recognizable listing, a release process, player accounts and a way to maintain the experience after launch. Adding payments or a shared economy brings further operational work.

Spawn's premise is that small teams should be able to spend more of their effort on the game itself. The platform would provide common services and a place for players to discover their work, while leaving room for different engines, art styles and game mechanics.

AI-assisted development makes clear integration rules especially useful. A creator working with a coding agent needs more than a downloadable library. They need a documented architecture, an example project, explicit service boundaries and a repeatable path from repository to playable build. Spawn's developer experience is being designed around that workflow.

## A library that grows through creators

The intended discovery experience takes inspiration from established game libraries: recognizable artwork, direct access to play, useful search and visible creator identities. Games should earn attention through the experience they offer. Spawn is starting with one real title rather than presenting a catalogue of hypothetical releases.

The initial focus is browser games. Supporting every engine, downloadable executable and server model at launch would expand the operational scope before the core publishing loop is proven. A narrow starting point allows the platform to learn from actual releases.

## Why a common economy

A shared token could let players use the same unit across participating games and give creators a common integration for approved purchases or rewards. Its usefulness would depend on what players can actually do with it, the quality of the games and the reliability of the platform.

This is a product hypothesis to validate. A token does not establish demand for a game, make an economy sustainable or remove the need to operate a trusted service. Economic parameters should follow observed use and a tested accounting model.

## What Spawn must demonstrate

The project has three early questions to answer: do players return to the games, can another developer integrate and publish successfully, and can shared services remain reliable as usage grows?

Evidence for those questions should come from completed integrations, player retention and measured operating costs. No audience size, revenue, funding or partner commitments are claimed in this version.
